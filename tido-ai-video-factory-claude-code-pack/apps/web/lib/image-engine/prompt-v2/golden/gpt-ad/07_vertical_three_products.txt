CULTURAL, REGIONAL AND SEASONAL SPECIFICS ARE YOUR JUDGEMENT, NOT THE SHEET'S.
Where the concept names a culture, a region, a festival, a season or an occasion, you must
do two things in SCENE & CONCEPT:

1. Name the EXACT visual identifiers — species, number of petals, colour, material, form,
   the way the thing is actually made or worn or arranged in that place.
2. Name the nearest neighbour that must be EXCLUDED, explicitly.

The failure this prevents, measured on real renders: a Vietnamese Tết brief asks for
*hoa mai*, a yellow five-petalled apricot blossom on a bare dark branch, and the render
comes back with pink Japanese cherry blossom — the most statistically common "festive
blossom" in any image model's training data. The two are not interchangeable and the
substitution is immediately obvious to the audience the asset is for. "Yellow apricot
blossom, five petals, on bare brown branches; NOT pink cherry blossom and NOT plum" is
the level of specificity required, in whatever the concept's own culture needs it.

The sheet deliberately contains no lookup table for this, and must never grow one: a table
would be wrong in a way nobody notices. You have read the concept. You do it.

## S3 — ARRANGEMENT AND DEPTH

  - The hero is a cup of iced milk tea, holding about half; nothing else competes for that share.
  - three depth layers: a soft near foreground, the hero sharp, a background clearly behind.
  - The group overlaps: items in front partly occlude those behind, so it reads as one object with depth, not a row.
  - Nothing is deformed, mirrored, stretched or duplicated to fill a gap.

## S4 — CAMERA

  - the lens a little above the subject's mid-line, looking down
  - the camera level, no tilt
  - a slightly long view, the background drawn closer
  - shallow focus holding the whole hero crisp, the background soft

## S5 — LIGHTING

  - a high key light from the front left, shadows falling back and right
  - the light is warm amber, like late afternoon
  - clearly modelled shadows, detail still readable
  - a narrow rim or edge light separates the subject from the background along one side
  - one light direction governs every highlight and shadow in the frame; no object casts a shadow in a direction the others do not

## S6 — SET AND PROPS

  - Surface: a plain matte surface a shade darker than the background, with no pattern of its own.
  - Background: a plain field of a warm off-white, close to unbleached paper, softly out of focus.
  - No added props beyond anything the concept names; otherwise the subject and the surface are the whole set.

## S7 — COLOUR

  - most of the frame is the field colour — a warm off-white, close to unbleached paper — carrying the background and the surface.
  - roughly a third is the secondary, a soft putty grey: the supporting surfaces and the larger shapes.
  - a small part is the accent, a deep black: emphasis only — a single element, a price card, the tightest highlight.
  - The background is never the product's own dominant colour.

## S8 — TYPOGRAPHY AND THE TEXT MANIFEST

  - One type family throughout: a clean, high-contrast sans-serif. Never more than two families in the frame.
  - the headline dominant, the supporting line a little over a third of it, the smallest line a third

  - no two items are identical copies: highlights, garnishes, fill levels and small marks all differ
