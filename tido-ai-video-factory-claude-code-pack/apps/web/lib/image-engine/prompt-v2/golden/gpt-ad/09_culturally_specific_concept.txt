Nothing in this section may be paraphrased, translated, corrected or improved. Where it
conflicts with anything below, it wins.

CULTURAL, REGIONAL AND SEASONAL SPECIFICS ARE YOUR JUDGEMENT, NOT THE SHEET'S.
Where the concept names a culture, a region, a festival, a season or an occasion, you must
do two things in SCENE & CONCEPT:

1. Name the EXACT visual identifiers — species, number of petals, colour, material, form,
   the way the thing is actually made or worn or arranged in that place.
2. Name the nearest neighbour that must be EXCLUDED, explicitly.

The failure this prevents, measured on real renders: a Vietnamese Tết brief asks for
*hoa mai*, a yellow five-petalled apricot blossom on a bare dark branch, and the render
comes back with pink Japanese cherry blossom — the most statistically common "festive
blossom" in any image model's training data. The two are not interchangeable and the
substitution is immediately obvious to the audience the asset is for. "Yellow apricot
blossom, five petals, on bare brown branches; NOT pink cherry blossom and NOT plum" is
the level of specificity required, in whatever the concept's own culture needs it.

The sheet deliberately contains no lookup table for this, and must never grow one: a table
would be wrong in a way nobody notices. You have read the concept. You do it.

## S1 — BIG IDEA

Trung thu in a northern Vietnamese courtyard: a star-shaped paper lantern, a plate of traditional mooncakes cut open to show the salted egg yolk, lacquered wood, late evening light. Not Chinese Mid-Autumn iconography and not Japanese lanterns.

Mood: warm, assured; addressed to whoever this poster is for, addressed directly and without hype

## S2 — LAYOUT AND ZONES

  - The frame is square.
  - a clear, even margin on every side, wide enough that nothing important reads as touching an edge; nothing that must be read crosses into it.
  - The subject occupies a little over half, in the middle band.
  - The words live in the top band, and nowhere else. No word is placed over the hero.
  - Leave roughly a quarter as quiet, uncluttered ground.

## S3 — ARRANGEMENT AND DEPTH

  - The hero is a traditional baked mooncake, cut to show the salted egg yolk, holding about half; nothing else competes for that share.
  - three depth layers: a soft near foreground, the hero sharp, a background clearly behind.
  - No overlap is needed: the subject stands clear of everything else.
  - Nothing is deformed, mirrored, stretched or duplicated to fill a gap.

## S4 — CAMERA

  - the lens a little above the subject's mid-line, looking down
  - the camera level, no tilt
  - a long, compressing view, the background flattened towards the subject
  - shallow focus: the hero crisp, the background soft

## S5 — LIGHTING

  - a high key light from the front left, shadows falling back and right
  - the light is warm amber, like late afternoon
  - clearly modelled shadows, detail still readable
  - a narrow rim or edge light separates the subject from the background along one side
  - one light direction governs every highlight and shadow in the frame; no object casts a shadow in a direction the others do not

## S6 — SET AND PROPS

  - Surface: wood, as the concept states.
  - Background: a plain field of a warm off-white, close to unbleached paper, softly out of focus.
  - No added props beyond anything the concept names; otherwise the subject and the surface are the whole set.

## S7 — COLOUR

  - most of the frame is the field colour — a warm off-white, close to unbleached paper — carrying the background and the surface.
  - roughly a third is the secondary, a soft putty grey: the supporting surfaces and the larger shapes.
  - a small part is the accent, a red: emphasis only — a single element, a price card, the tightest highlight.
  - The background is never the product's own dominant colour.

## S8 — TYPOGRAPHY AND THE TEXT MANIFEST

  - One type family throughout: a clean, high-contrast sans-serif. Never more than two families in the frame.
  - the headline dominant, the supporting line a little over a third of it, the smallest line a third
  - small text sits on a flat card or a plain untextured area — never over the product, never over texture
  - Every Vietnamese diacritic is rendered exactly as written, in NFC. No string is added, shortened, translated or corrected.

  THE TEXT MANIFEST — every string that may appear, each exactly once:
    [headline] "Trung thu đoàn viên" — at the top of the text zone, first in the reading order, tall enough to read at a glance, set in a red
    [price line] "Đặt bánh trước 20/8" — grouped with the other numbered lines, on a flat card inside the text zone, tall enough to read at a glance, set in a red

  Nothing outside this manifest is set. No extra numerals, no invented strap line, no watermark.

## S9 — REALISM AND FINISH

  - every object meets the surface with a contact shadow, darkest where they touch, plus a faint reflection where the surface is glossy
  - focus falls away outward from the hero, not uniformly
  - asymmetric micro-imperfections: an unmirrored droplet, a crumb out of line, real variation in the material
  - true scale between objects
  - no waxy, plastic or over-smoothed surfaces, no HDR halo
  - Finish: a gentle S-curve in the tone, highlights rolling off rather than clipping, blacks that keep detail, no sharpening halo.

## S10 — CONSTRAINTS

  - no text, numeral or character other than the strings listed
  - no logo, wordmark, emblem or icon beyond what the print rule allows
  - no duplicate copy of any product
  - no watermark, signature, frame or border inside the image
  - no raw or undercooked food unless the concept asks for it
  - no inedible props touching the food
  - Not Chinese Mid-Autumn iconography and not Japanese lanterns

## S11 — DERIVED DECISIONS (why the sheet says what it says; never written into the prompt)

  - asset family: poster; canvas: 1:1

**REFERENCE IMAGES** names every attached image by number — "Image 1 is …" — with its
