A square skincare poster, designed as one finished piece.

The two products are exactly the ones in the attached photos: photo 1 is the tall bottle of
honey-amber cleansing oil with a white pump, photo 2 is the shorter clear glass dropper bottle
with a white rubber-bulb cap. Their shapes, colours and printed labels stay exactly as
photographed, and nothing else is written on them.

The idea: the oil is the sunrise. Two bottles stand side by side on a thick slab of white
marble with grey veins, set in a shallow pool of perfectly still water, and the only thing that
moves is a ring of ripples spreading from the slab's edge across the lower third. Morning sun
enters from behind-left through a window out of frame and passes through the oil, so the amber
bottle glows like a lantern and throws a warm patch of amber light onto the marble in front of
it; the clear ampoule shows bright edges and a thin line of light through its glass. Three fresh
centella leaves drift on the water, one close to the viewer and softly out of focus.

Behind the bottles, a pale sage-grey wall catches the soft diagonal shadow of a window frame.
The wall is calm and evenly toned across the top third, and all the lettering sits there,
centred. The bottles are centred and fill about half the height, sharp from cap to base; the
water and leaves in the foreground and the wall behind are softer.

Colours: honey amber as the dominant glow, white marble and sage wall as support, leaf green as
the single accent.

Lettering is a light, wide-spaced sans-serif in deep green-grey, flat and crisp, and never
touches the bottles. First, a small line of capitals:
"BỘ ĐÔI CHÂN ÁI TỪ RAU MÁ MADAGASCAR"
Below it, the headline in two lines, the largest text:
"ĐÁNH THỨC VẺ ĐẸP NGUYÊN BẢN CỦA LÀN DA"

Finish: a quiet Korean skincare editorial photograph, natural light, fine natural texture, not
a CGI render.

No other text, no extra logos or brand marks, no people. Each line of text appears once.
1:1 aspect ratio.
